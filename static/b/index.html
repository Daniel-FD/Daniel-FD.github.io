Abstract

I'm a data scientist in London. I lead data science at Range, following stablecoins across 21 chains and building the risk models behind $30B+ in monitored assets. Part-time, I'm a research associate at Imperial, using machine learning to find biomarkers in allergy trials. Before all that I spent four years at Imperial studying how fluids move around things, which is what Figure 1 is about. The thread, if there is one: fluids, then data, then money. Everything flows somewhere.

3Methods

Figure 1 solves the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on your GPU, every frame, in a few hundred lines of WebGL. The velocity is first carried along by itself (semi-Lagrangian advection), then made divergence-free by solving a Poisson equation for the pressure and subtracting its gradient:

∇²p = ∇·u*, u = u* − ∇p. (1)

Equation (1) is relaxed with Jacobi iteration (Listing 1); the letters impose ∂p/∂n = 0 and no through-flow. Vorticity confinement returns some of the swirl the grid smears away. Smoke enters through a rake at the left edge and is advected with a MacCormack scheme, which keeps the streaks sharp far downstream. The viscosity is purely numerical, so the Reynolds number is nominal.
